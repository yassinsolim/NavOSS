Provenance and limitations

Know where the map comes from

Map and place data

NavOSS uses OpenStreetMap-derived map and place data together with The City of Calgary's public Business Licenses and Parcel Address datasets. OpenStreetMap is open data licensed under the Open Data Commons Open Database License. Visible attribution names both OpenStreetMap contributors and The City of Calgary.

Calgary records are mirrored to a private local index every 24 hours; live searches do not go to Calgary Open Data. Home-occupation business records and malformed parcel rows are excluded. No source can guarantee every informal, new, relocated, or unlicensed place, and parcel coordinates may not identify the best vehicle entrance.

Map display

The app draws the map with MapLibre from OpenFreeMap vector tiles, which are built from OpenStreetMap data, and shows the required OpenStreetMap attribution on the map. The illustrations on this website are original artwork with invented streets; they are not map data or screenshots of a real place.

Routing

Production routes are calculated by self-hosted Valhalla using the same checksum-verified Alberta and British Columbia OpenStreetMap release as self-hosted Nominatim. Current live certification covers Calgary and Kelowna local routes plus both intercity directions. Route responses remain explicit that live traffic is unavailable.

Province boundaries (not yet in the deployed service)

The updated server code decides what is inside the Alberta and British Columbia service area from Statistics Canada's 2021 Census province and territory digital boundary files (PRUID 48 and 59, published 2022-09-21), under the Open Government Licence – Canada. The polygons are simplified to about 0.001 degrees (up to roughly 110 m) and include coastal waters and islands, so they are not a survey-grade or border-control geofence. Community road reports and traffic (not switched on)

Community road reports and community traffic come from NavOSS drivers who choose to share them; they are not official data and are labelled as such. Neither is switched on in the deployed service, and no released app sends them. Traffic road shapes and identifiers come from OpenStreetMap through the self-hosted Valhalla graph. When traffic is shown it is attributed “Traffic from opted-in NavOSS drivers” and published only where at least three installations drove a road. NavOSS reviewed official Alberta and British Columbia feeds on November 12, 2026 and found none with free, redistributable, real-time road speeds, so none is used. See the privacy policy for what is collected.

Offline map pipeline (not available to users)

The repository contains a build pipeline for downloadable Alberta and British Columbia map, routing and search bundles. No bundle is hosted and the app cannot download one yet. It uses public, dated OpenStreetMap extracts from Geofabrik, not OpenFreeMap's public endpoint. If bundles ship, their attribution travels with them; the components and licences are:

Safety cameras

Fixed red-light and speed-on-green locations come from The City of Calgary's Intersection Safety Cameras dataset. NavOSS validates and caches the official records without sending user location to Calgary Open Data.

Kelowna regional context

Official Kelowna-area construction, closures, and incidents come from DriveBC Open511. Ordinary highway webcam locations and image links come from the OGL-BC DriveBC HighwayCams catalogue. These webcams are not enforcement cameras. Fixed public police facility markers come from the official Kelowna RCMP contact directory and are not live officer, patrol, incident, or checkpoint locations.

Kelowna search and driving routes use the live, separately certified regional OpenStreetMap import. DriveBC events and webcams are informational overlays and never alter route selection, ETA, rerouting, or speech.

Camera data can be incomplete or outdated. Alerts are supplementary and never replace posted signs or road laws.

What NavOSS does not use

NavOSS does not scrape or copy route, place, traffic, or map data from Apple Maps, Google Maps, Waze, or other proprietary navigation products. Manual comparisons are evidence for testing, not a source database.

An optional Google Places UI Kit component can receive the selected public POI name and coordinate and render Google's photos, rating and rating count, review text, and required attribution in its own visually separated native view. NavOSS checks the returned name and coordinate to prevent a mismatch, but does not add those values to its OpenStreetMap result, read them into shared contracts, or retain them. GooglePlacesSwift 10.15.0's underlying SDK privacy manifest declares location, Device ID, Other Data, performance, product interaction, and search history collection for analytics and/or app functionality, with no tracking. The separate external reviews link opens Google Maps only after the user chooses it. Builds without the restricted Google key make no Google place-details request and do not link that SDK.
